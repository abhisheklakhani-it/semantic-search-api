# Looking after an electric car battery

## Charging habits

Lithium-ion batteries age fastest when they sit at a very high or very low state of charge for long periods. For daily driving, most manufacturers recommend charging to about 80 percent and keeping 100 percent for long trips. Leaving the car parked for weeks at full charge speeds up capacity loss.

DC fast charging is convenient on motorways, but frequent fast charging raises cell temperature and can slowly reduce capacity. Slow AC charging at home or at work is gentler on the battery.

## Temperature

Heat is the main enemy of battery life. Park in the shade in summer and use preconditioning, which heats or cools the battery while the car is still plugged in. In winter a cold battery accepts charge more slowly and the range drops, often by 20 to 30 percent, because energy is spent on heating the cabin and the pack.

## Warranty and degradation

Most traction batteries are covered by a warranty of eight years or 160,000 km, with a guaranteed minimum of about 70 percent of the original capacity. Typical real-world degradation is one to two percent per year.
